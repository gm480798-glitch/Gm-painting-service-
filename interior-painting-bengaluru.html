Preparation: putty, primer and wall repair

A good interior paint job depends mostly on what happens before the first coat. Depending on the wall, preparation can include:

  • Scraping loose or flaking paint and cleaning dust and stains.
  • Filling cracks and dents, and applying putty where the wall surface needs levelling.
  • Sanding for a smooth surface.
  • Applying primer so the paint grips evenly.

The estimate should say clearly which of these steps are included for your walls.

Choosing paint and finish for each room

We can work with suitable branded paint systems such as Asian Paints, Berger, Dulux and Nerolac, depending on your requirement. Bedrooms and living rooms usually suit a soft, even finish. Kitchen walls need extra attention to grease and cleaning before painting. Discuss colour, sheen and paint quality before approval, and consider a texture or feature wall for one accent area.

Wall paint peeling, putty damage and damp patches

Peeling paint is often caused by moisture behind the wall, poor surface preparation or an unsuitable earlier coat. Repainting over it without fixing the cause usually brings the problem back.

Putty damage shows up as flaking, powdery or blistered patches. The loose material is removed and the area is re-prepared before painting.

Damp patches and seepage should be diagnosed first. Paint alone does not stop a leak, so see our waterproofing and wall seepage treatment page.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the paint peeling on my wall?

Common causes are dampness, a weak or dusty surface, or poor preparation before the last coat. An inspection helps identify the cause before repainting.

Do I need putty before repainting?

It depends on the wall. Walls with cracks, dents or damaged old putty usually need it; walls in good condition may only need cleaning, sanding and primer.

Can you paint only one wall or one room?

Yes. Single-wall and few-room projects are possible; the price depends on area, surface and finish.

Can I repaint a wall that has damp patches?

The dampness source should be found and treated first, otherwise the new paint can blister or peel again.
